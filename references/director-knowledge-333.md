# Director Knowledge from 333 Focus Media TVCs

Use this reference after the advertising message is locked and before choosing the director mechanism.

## Contents

- Evidence boundary and director formula

## Evidence Boundary

The project contains 333 delivered TVCs, 333 copy records, 3,431 shot rows, and 333 reverse-generation prompts. The pattern library is a rule-assisted primary classification, not a frame-by-frame human verdict on every film. Use it as a strong prior and retrieval map, not a mechanical answer key.

Machine-readable sources bundled with this skill:

- `333-visual-event-patterns.json`: all 333 primary-event classifications.
- `333-high-value-examples.csv`: 36 high-value examples with category, carrier, mechanism, and transferable lesson.

Search these files only when the brief needs a relevant precedent. Do not load all cases into every task.

## Director Formula

```text
Advertising-message type
→ visible category carrier
→ memorable event operation
→ product causal role
→ 2-3 escalating repetitions
→ brand attribution
```

A carrier is the thing the audience can see or hear repeatedly. An event is what the carrier does. A useful director idea needs both.

Do not select the event operation from slogan keywords alone. First understand the category behavior, product action, available brand assets, audience situation, and what can become a visible carrier. In the V0.2.1 copy-only replay, keyword selection over-assigned rhythmization to 227 of 333 films, while the canonical library contains 137 rhythmization cases. This is evidence that the library must remain a retrieval aid rather than a closed decision engine.

## Three Director Layers

Approve all three layers before execution:

1. **Communication strategy**: what should be remembered, in which future situation, and why this product is the answer.
2. **Creative mechanism**: what carrier experiences what event, why the event is surprising or escalating, and how the product causes it.
3. **Cinematic execution**: how action, reaction, performance, material, camera, edit, and sound make the mechanism legible rather than posed.

A structurally correct mechanism can still produce a lifeless film when the third layer is missing.

## Known Event Operations

These eight operations summarize recurring patterns in the current corpus. They are neither exhaustive nor mutually exclusive. Record the mechanism in open language first; add a known-operation label only when it helps retrieval or pressure-testing. A route outside this library is valid when it passes the same causality, memory, attribution, and execution gates.

| Operation | Evidence | Use when | Director requirement | Reject when |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Rhythmization | 137 / 41.1% | jingle, repeated brand/product name, many SKUs or benefits | bind each beat to product, package, action, or claim; return to brand | rhythm becomes unrelated story or text wall |
| Function made visible | 65 / 19.5% | invisible cleaning, protection, freshness, intelligence, safety | show problem, product intervention, function path, changed result | generic technology glow with no product causality |
| Standard challenge | 55 / 16.5% | first, leading, years, certification, numeric proof | turn the standard into a visible test or challenge | awards, certificates, and giant numbers replace proof |
| Causal trigger | 33 / 9.9% | pour, open, press, spray, wear, choose | product action visibly triggers a result and can repeat | product is posed but causes nothing |
| Material transformation | 17 / 5.1% | fragrant, crisp, smooth, warm, fresh, rich | make product material move or transform into a sensory result | only faces, atmosphere, or free-floating effects express sensation |
| Relationship action | 14 / 4.2% | family, children, gifts, companionship, sharing | product improves a concrete giving, eating, walking, opening, or sharing action | people merely smile while product is background |
| Path journey | 8 / 2.4% | origin, craft, heritage, age, terroir | let origin/craft travel through product action to user benefit | film stops at field, workshop, plaque, or history mood |
| Symbol replacement | 4 / 1.2% | celebrity, IP, mascot, brand character | character performs or triggers product action and returns memory to product | audience remembers character but not product; small-sample prior only |

## Selection Heuristics

- For a jingle, consider rhythmization before a complex story, but reject it when the product has a stronger visible behavior or the rhythm would reduce every film to the same template.
- For numeric or authority copy, begin with a standard challenge or function made visible rather than a giant-text answer.
- For product-action copy, ask what visible change the action triggers.
- For sensory copy, return to product material and use action.
- For craft/origin copy, travel through product into a present-day benefit.
- For relationship copy, define the concrete relationship action and how the product improves it.
- Combine at most one primary and one or two supporting operations. Do not stack all mechanisms.
- When direction is open, generate at least one route from relevant precedents and one route derived directly from the product/category tension without naming a library operation first.

## Creative Tension and Performance Gate

For each proposed route, state:

- **tension or contrast**: what expectation, problem, social truth, sensory contradiction, or scale difference creates interest;
- **creative leap**: what makes the treatment more than a literal illustration of the slogan;
- **trigger**: what starts the event;
- **action-reaction chain**: what each person or object does next and why;
- **performance objective**: what a person wants in the moment, not merely the expression they should wear;
- **handoff**: how one action creates the next shot or beat;
- **product necessity**: why the event cannot happen in the same way without this product;
- **asset leverage**: whether a supplied celebrity, IP, product form, interface, location, sound, or pack is already a stronger memory carrier than a new invented device;
- **category specificity**: which material, behavior, proof, scene, or cinematic choice could not be swapped freely with a competitor;
- **duration economy**: how many beats the actual runtime can support; short films may need one compressed event rather than a full six-beat story;
- **claim truth**: whether visualization clarifies an approved fact without implying an unsupported product mechanism or result;
- **generation reality**: whether the chosen model and available references can express the spatial and performance logic reliably.

Reject routes built mainly from smiling, pointing, holding, posing, looking at camera, generic lifestyle montage, or atmosphere without motivated action.

Do not assume the most dramatic or magical route is automatically best. Prefer direct product proof, existing brand assets, or a dense product page when they communicate more credibly and efficiently for the runtime and category.

## Memory Encoding Gate

Ask five questions before approving the Director Contract:

1. **CEP binding**: In what future buying or usage situation should the audience recall this film?
2. **Distinctive asset cue**: Which brand-owned color, package, logo, sound, character, phrase, or action enables attribution?
3. **Product causality**: Does the product cause the visible/audible change, or could any competitor replace it?
4. **Escalating repetition**: Can the event repeat two or three times with increasing information or impact?
5. **Brand attribution**: Does the ending close the event back onto the brand and product?

Product-causality failure blocks execution. Brand-attribution failure requires revision or an explicit pressure test. Other weak items may proceed only when recorded in the Director Contract.

The V0.2.1 333 replay also found repeatable evidence on both sides:

- delivered films often contribute cinematic shot variety, material specificity, offer density, and performed interaction that a mechanism-only blueprint misses;
- the pipeline can improve many delivered structures by adding visible action progression, replacing poses with action-reaction, reducing text dependence, strengthening product causality, and closing brand attribution.

Treat these as review lenses, not automatic verdicts about which film is better. A structural proxy cannot judge acting, taste, humor, or final generated-video quality.

## Creative Levels

- **L3 execution**: clear claim, product presence, usable 15-second structure.
- **L4 memorable event**: one repeated carrier/event that can be recalled and attributed.
- **L5 brand asset**: the event or fluent device can recur across future films and remain brand-owned.

Do not force every task into L5. Match ambition to campaign role, evidence, budget, and generation controllability.

## One-Sentence Test

```text
This film makes people in [future situation] recall [brand + product] through [carrier/event], because [product-caused change], and closes attribution with [brand asset].
```

If the sentence is vague, the director direction is not ready for storyboard or audio execution.
